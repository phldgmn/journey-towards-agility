 2. Background
 - engineering of software
 for ubiquitous
 systems
 Ubiquitous
 computing,
 as envisioned

 (1993)

 than 20 years

 com- puters

 In the years

 of ubicomp
 systems
 are
 related

 for software
 engineer-
 ing advances
 to support the research
 endeavors
 in ubiquitous
 computing

 1999
 ). This need
 has led

 ing researchers
 to propose,
 over
 the last
 15 years
 or so, a large
 number
 of approaches
 for different
 phases of the engineering pro-
 cess speciﬁcally
 tailored
 for the characteristics
 of ubiquitous
 sys-
 tems
 ( Abowd,
 2012
 ). 2.1.
 Summary of previous

 Reviews,

 for ubiquitous

 ). 2.2. Objectives

 The area

 on evidence

 about approaches
 and their limitations for developing

 for ubiquitous

 This problem,
 we think,
 could be greatly

 if researchers
 in this area
 fol- lowed
 systematic

 and strived
 to apply

 to real
 scenarios, including more

 for
 research
 and practice

 review
 can serve

 to get a quite

 for engi- neering of software
 for ubiquitous
 systems.
 The results
 and studies
 presented
 in the papers listed
 in this review
 might not
 be in some cases directly
 applicable in industry,
 but they
 can be useful to feed
 the reﬂections
 when looking for solutions during the process
 of development.
 For
 research,
 the review
 shows
 that while some phases of the development
 cycle
 have
 received
 much
 attention
 due to their im- portance
 (viz., feedback
 and evolution/maintenance)

 prac-

 impor-

 still
 need
 more
 and better

 on the review
 researchers
 can direct
 their research
 more
 precisely

 ing to the open issues identiﬁed
 and research
 efforts
 repartition

 described
 in this review.
 5.4. Limitations
 of this review
 The main limitations of this review
 relate
 to publication selec- tion bias, inaccuracy
 in data extraction,
 and misclassiﬁcation. To avoid
 selection bias we use a hybrid
 setup
 for the search
 processes
 that guided
 the initial selection of studies
 based
 on the literature
 rather
 than only
 on our experience
 and knowledge.
 At this respect
 we also performed
 three
 separate
 search
 processes
 of different
 type each.
 Finally,
 we distributed
 the work
 load in such
 a way
 that the search
 and selection tasks
 were
 performed
 by more
 than one researcher
 and checked
 by one researcher
 not
 involved
 in the original process.
 This last
 point was
 aimed
 also at increasing
 the accuracy
 in data extraction.
 However,
 the data extraction
 was
 highly
 dependent on the way
 studies
 were
 reported
 and on how
 authors
 of the different
 papers explained
 concepts
 and methodolo-
 gies, as well
 as the kind of terminology

 This in many

 relevant
 information, which
 may
 have
 derived

 of inaccuracy

 over

 based
 on what was
 found on the selected

 subjective
 in that it depends on how
 the original authors
 decided
 to present
 their results
 and what we could consider rele-
 vant
 on them. 6. Conclusion On searching
 the literature
 for papers that present
 work
 on the development
 of software
 for ubiquitous
 systems
 we identiﬁed
 5432 papers. From
 these, we found 128
 papers presenting
 one or more
 approaches
 each,
 exhibiting
 acceptable
 rigor,
 credibility,
 and relevance.
 The total
 number
 of approaches
 found was
 132.
 We classify the approaches
 based
 on two
 levels,
 one depending on the phase in the software
 development
 life
 cycle
 to which
 they
 contribute
 and, two,
 depending on the focus or main concern the approach
 is aimed
 to address.
 Our classiﬁcation was
 predominantly
 data driven
 in order
 to avoid
 bias from
 previous
 knowledge,
 letting
 the works
 from
 the literature
 be the ones leading the results
 of the review.
 From
 the papers selected,
 we could identify eight different
 phases in the development
 cycle
 which
 answered
 RQ1.
 Most
 ap- proaches
 found were
 addressing
 issues within one of the eight phases and a small number
 of approaches
 aimed
 to address
 the development
 life
 cycle
 as a whole. Then, we screened
 all selected
 papers, phase by phase, to identify the main approaches
 (RQ2) and the limitations recognized
 by the authors
 of the approaches
 for each
 phase (RQ3). Finally,
 we have
 collected
 the issues that have
 been
 highlighted
 in the literature
 as still
 open for further investi-
 gation
 (RQ4). We found that further research
 related
 to software
 for ubiq- uitous
 systems
 is needed
 around
 the concept
 of context,
 runtime support, user interaction,
 how
 to provide
 adequate
 support for de- velopers,
 and security and privacy.
 Research
 effort
 s need
 to fo- cus also on self-adaptation
 and the increasingly
 important
 role
 that machine
 learning has in the development
 of adaptive
 sys-
 tems.
 Aspects related
 to systems’
 performance and operation,
 as well
 as how
 pervasive
 systems
 can beneﬁt
 from
 data extraction
 and analysis
 at runtime should be further explored.
 Furthermore,
 it is necessary
 to explore
 techniques
 and methodologies
 that al- low
 users with technical
 and non-technical
 backgrounds
 to un- derstand
 the functioning of ubiquitous
 systems
 at runtime in or-
 der to enhance the results
 that systems
 can offer
 and the expe-
 rience that users may
 have
 when interacting
 with these type of systems.
 Based
 on our review
 we can also say
 that the quality
 of experi-
 mentations
 and case studies
 deﬁnitions in current
 research
 of soft-
 ware
 engineering for ubiquitous
 systems
 need
 to be improved.
 It is necessary
 to make
 cases more
 reproducible
 and closer to real
 life
 cases. We recommend
 that researchers
 follow
 existing
 guidelines for conducting case studies
 (e.g., Yin, 2013
