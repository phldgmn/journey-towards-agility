Introduction
 Since the early
 1990s,
 software
 architecture
 has been
 subject of increasing
 interest
 in software
 engineering research
 and practice.
 Three
 classic reasons
 why
 architecture
 is important
 to software
 systems
 are:
 (1) architecture
 provides
 a basis for creating
 mutual understanding
 about the software
 system;
 (2) architecture
 design decisions have
 the most
 signiﬁcant inﬂuence on system
 qualities;
 and (3) architecture
 provides
 a reusable
 abstraction
 of a system
 that is transferable
 to other
 systems
 with similar requirements
 promoting
 large-scale
 reuse.
 In the last
 decade, the awareness
 has grown
 that the decision making process
 is central
 to architectural
 design, as manifested
 in the notions
 of rational
 and architecture
 knowledge.
 Despite
 the maturity of the ﬁeld, software
 architecture
 does not
 escape from
 the impact
 of the ever-changing
 world
 in which
 we live.
 The need
 for on the ﬂy re-conﬁgurability
 and self-adaptation
 to deal with dynamic operating
 conditions and new
 emerging
 user requirements
 poses severe
 challenges
 to software
 architects.
 New developments
 such
 as the cloud, virtualization, big data, ultra-large
 scale systems,
 long-lived
 systems
 and systems-of-systems
 require
 new
 architecture
 solutions. The aim of this special issue is to doc- ument new
 frontiers
 in software
 architecture
 and outline the chal-
 lenges
 for the coming years.
 This issue The idea for this special issue arose
 from
 conversations
 with the organizing
 committee
 of the Ninth European
 Conference
 on Software
 Architecture
 (ECSA),
 which
 was
 held in Cavtat/Dubrovnik,
 Croatia,
 September
 2015.
 The response
 to the call for papers for this special issue drew
 heavily
 from
 the ECSA
 community,
 but also attracted
 submissions more
 broadly.
 We received
 seventeen
 sub- missions for this special issue. These papers were
 rigorously
 re-
 viewed
 by an international
 group
 of experts
 in the ﬁeld of software
 architectures.

 on these reviews,
 we were
 able to recommend

 Intru- sion Detection,”by
 Adriana
 Leite

 reason-

 formance of the agent

 ments to detect
 intrusions in dynamic environments

 In particular,
 the article illustrates
 the experiences
 and lessons learned
 from
 two
 applications of the approach
 and offers
 re-
 search
 directions
 for improving
 education
 on architecting
 in ag- ile projects.
 –“Exploiting

 Traceability

 The approach

 intro-

 the re-
 trieved
