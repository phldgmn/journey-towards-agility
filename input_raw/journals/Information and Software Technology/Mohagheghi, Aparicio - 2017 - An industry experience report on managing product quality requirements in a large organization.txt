 1. Introduction
 The term

 Others have

 requirements

 we use the term
 “quality
 requirements”for
 concerns other
 than functionality,
 while development
 constraints
 (such
 as time, budget
 and technical
 environment)
 are
 out of the scope of the work.

 2. Background
 and motivation
 NAV
 administers
 a large
 proportion
 of welfare
 beneﬁts
 and social security schemes
 in Norway.
 These include almost
 50 types of beneﬁts
 that cover
 different
 stages

 unemployment

 2.8 million users, NAV

 Over
 400

 as to 19,0
 0 0 employees

 NAV

 With the vision of offering

 When participating in development,

 New development

 NAV’s

 They

 The practice

 This is however

 NAV’s

 Since 2012,

 . Some extra

 Examples

 NAV

 4. Attainable

 Many

 Following

 3. Literature

 This section presents

 3.1.
 Requirement
 elicitation Requirement
 elicitation has been
 subject of extensive
 research
 and over
 time, several
 requirement
 elicitation techniques
 have
 been
 proposed.
 Examples
 are
 interview-based
 methods,
 proto-
 typing, speciﬁcation by example,
 observing users, task
 analysis
 and requirement
 workshops
 [36,42] . The distinction
 between
 functional and quality
 requirements
 exists
 in most
 requirement
 classiﬁcation methods;
 see for example
 [26,30,36] . Several
 studies
 have
 shown
 the signiﬁcance of quality
 requirements
 in software
 projects
 as well
 [8,16,23,30,36]
 . Capturing
 qualities

 for capturing
 functional requirements
 or other
 more
 speciﬁc approaches,

 Engineering Institute’s
 Architecture
 TradeoffAnalysisMethod
 (ATAM)
 [4] and Quality Attribute
 Workshops
 (QAW)
 [5] . Other techniques
 such

 mapping [2] may
 be applied
 as well;
 which
 is a tech-
 nique
 that helps organizations
 describe
 their goals,
 communicate
 assumptions
 and develop
 roadmaps
 to achieve
 the goals.
 Conﬂict identiﬁcation and resolution
 is part of requirement
 elicitation since stakeholders’
 interests
 and priorities are
 different.
 Conﬂict resolu-
 tion may
 specially
 be challenging
 for quality
 requirements
 since dependencies between
 quality
 requirements
 are
 often
 complex
 and some requirements
 may
 even
 not
 be compatible
 with others.
 To identify which
 stakeholders
 to notify
 of a potential
 conﬂict, a stakeholder
 / quality-requirement
 relationship
 map may
 be useful that shows
 which
 stakeholders
 are
 interested
 in which
 quality
 requirements
 [25] . Results
 of a case study
 on 15 digital library
 systems
 in university
 environment
 presented
 in [25] showed
 that in case of conﬂicts of interests,
 a tendency
 exists
 to gravitate
 to satisfactory
 solutions and not
 necessarily
 optimal
 solutions unless the issue is at the core
 of a stakeholder
 interest.
 There
 has also been
 focus on capturing
 quality
 requirements
 early
 in the development
 process
 since some requirements
 drive
 architectural
 decisions; thus the term
 “Architecturally
 Signiﬁcant requirements
 (ASRs)”has
 emerged
 [11,12]
 . Chen et al. deﬁne ASRs as requirements

 impact

 architecture
 [11]
 . “Signiﬁcant”indicates

 of change.
 ASRs have
 wide impact,
 target

 are

 and are
 often

 In practice,
 many

 fail

 quality
 concerns. For

 Franch

 such

 [21]
 . Interesting

 Quality requirements

 lated

 interoperability,
 availability
 , and to a lesser extent

 In more

 3.2. Classiﬁcation of quality

 Software

 Klas et al. distinguish

 quality.
 This work
 has an organizational
 perspective
 that extends
 beyond
 single projects.

 8. Discussion This section positions the results
 described
 in this paper against
 related
 work
 and presents

 Elicitation of quality

 We performed

 with pre-analysis,
 post-detailing

 from
 stakeholders.
 Projects

 other
 method
 for requirement

 In conducting requirement

 Mapping technique
 [2] . Impact

 ; i.e. who will be impacted

 we have
 used
 the term

 product,
 either positively
 or negatively.
 The second level
 of an impact

 that will be created.
 In our approach
 the various
 quality
 characteristics
 represent

 impact.
 The third
 level
 of an impact
 map focuses on what must

 i.e. Deliverables
 . In a quality
 model, deliverables

 impact.
 The resulting
 Impact
 Map is the sum- mary

 Gojko
 Adzic,
 the creator

 Mapping, discusses typical mistakes

 many
 people, wrong
 people, criticizing too
 early,
 reverse
 engineering (knowing
 the desired
 output and rejecting
 other
 options),
 and going
 into
 too
 much
 low-level
 detail
 early
 [2] . These are
 warnings
 that we agree
 with. When creating
 and detailing
 quality
 requirements,
 a common challenge
 is to specify measurable
 requirements
 accompanied
 with veriﬁcation
 scenarios. Involving
 stakeholders
 such

 (espe- cially

 tests)
 and maintenance
 experts

 elicitation is important

 scenarios. In NAV,
 the quality
 requirement
 test
 team
 has gained
 lots
 of expertise
 on acceptance
 testing.
 Reusing
 lists
 of requirements
 facilitates
 the requirement
 elicitation process
 as well,
 while some requirements
 must
 be tailored
 to speciﬁc project
 contexts.
 Doer et al. call such

 for experience-based
 artefacts
 [16]
 . Another
 challenge
 is communication of requirements
 and knowledge

 [42] . Loss of knowledge
 in product
 handover
 be-
 tween
 projects
 and maintenance
 staffor
 contractors
 is a common problem.
 Long-living

 development
 and staffturnoverscall

 Collecting a manageable
