Bene˜t estimation for a story should relate to objectives 
not directly but indirectly, through the bene˜t points (BPs) 
of that story™s epic. (Epics are high-level requirements 
perceived at the project™s early stages, typically at project 

inception. In contrast, stories are more re˜ned and elabo
-rated user stories re˚ecting the project™s understanding at 

more advanced stages.) Previously, we suggested a syntax 

for epics that explicitly mentioned objectives.

to think of stories in terms of their contribution to their 
epic, you can use the following syntax, which avoids men

Epics can be very high level and represent large chunks 
of functionality. When distributing BPs from the epic, you 
might be faced with more stories than you can comfortably 
keep track of. The solution? Adapt distribution techniques 

based on assessing the relative importance.
Our analysis of four techniques
2,3
 led us to recommend 
pairwise comparison facilitated by the analytical hierarchy 
process (AHP),
4 which is easily implemented in a tool. The 
basis for AHP is that comparing many items in one go is ex
-tremely taxing cognitively. Instead, consider only two items 
(stories) at a time by assessing their relative importance to 

their epic.
But doesn™t this extremely local procedure completely 
ignore the whole picture and all the relationships between 

items? Yes and no. From your judgments on two items at 
a time, AHP deduces a ranking of all the items, even in the 
face of your inconsistencies. For example, unless you have 

extraordinary capabilities, your collection of local pairwise 
comparisons will likely imply that, for some
 stories 
EX and 
EY, both 
EX > EY and 
EY > EX. AHP computes a measure of 
this inconsistencyŠthe 
consistency index
 (CI). In line with 
satis˜cing rather than optimizing,
5 you can make an edu
-cated choice as to a ﬁgood enoughﬂ CI.
In standard AHP, all possible pairs of stories in an epic 
must be compared, which can be ˜ne for a moderate num
-ber of stories. In our experiments, we implemented a method 
to reduce the number of required comparisons
6 (with the 
penalty of having to be more consistent) so that you can use 

AHP for a large number of stories.
Pairwise comparison is a core element of judgment cog
-nitive processes.
