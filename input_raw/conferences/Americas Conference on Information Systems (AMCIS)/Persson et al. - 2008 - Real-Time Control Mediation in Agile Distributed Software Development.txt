ABSTRACT
Agile distributed environments pose particular challenges related to control of quality and collaboration in software 
development. Moreover, while face
-to
-face interaction is fundamental in agile development, distributed environments must 
rely
 extensively on mediated interactions. On this backdrop, we report from an in
-depth case study of an agile distributed 
software project. Applying Kirsch™s elements of control framework, we analyze how actors in this context used different 

elements of contr
ol. We offer a description of the general management context and 
provide
 a detailed analysis of how control 
was mediated 
over distance 
by technology 
through 
real
-time
 exchanges
. 
Contrary to previous research, the analysis suggests 
that both formal and info
rmal elements of 
real
-time mediated 
control were used; that evolving goals 
and adjustment of 
expectations were two
 of the main issues 
in real
-time mediated control exchanges
; and, that the actors, despite distances in 
space and culture, developed a clan
-li
ke pattern 
mediated by technology 
to help control quality and collaboration in software 
development.
Keywords
Distributed development, global software development, control theory.

mutual dependence among 
team members
. The clan pattern is not surprising considering it i

(Harris et al., 2006)
