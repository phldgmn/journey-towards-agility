 Abstract 
 The rise of Software as a Service (SaaS) pla
tforms is driven by the 
current trend towards
 customer
-oriented, agile and scalable service provisioning. CustomersÕ
 demand for highly specialized services 
can mostly not be met by a single provider that offers a holistic solution but 
by a consortium of 
providers that cooperate. 
In order to enable the combination of services and hence, the collaboration 
between different service providers, technical challenges like the compatibility of interfaces have to be 
dealt with. 
In addition
, economic issues concerning profit, reliability an
d risk
 of service failure
 have to 
be taken into consideration.
 A model of Agreement Networks
 (ANs)
 is presented 
that illustrate
 the
 legal bindings
 that are 
established or under negotiation
 between

 a methodology
 is introduced

supp

of 
Service Level 
Agreements (
SLAs

 Finally, a benchmark 
for
 risk
-based 
decision
s is derived
 that serves to evaluate 
the efficiency of the choice. 
The impact of the available 
amount of monitoring data on the decision is investigated and a minimal amount of 100 observations 
for each SLA combination is suggested.

 7 Conclusion
 In this paper, 
the model of Agreement Networks 
was proposed
 and a
 decision support model 
for
 service providers that enables
 the selection of SLA combinations 
was introduced that 
allows for 
the
 providerÕs
 risk
-awareness. 
Risk 
of SLA violation 
in this context is expressed
 analogously to security 
portfolio risk stemming from finance.
 This approach enables a service provider to 
select the 
combination of SLAs 
based 
upon past observations that
 is expected to incur the lowest risk of SLA 
violation 
on the provider while 
meetin
g a pre
-defined expected profit constraint.
 Note that
 our 
approach might reject combinations of SLAs
, which combine above average expected profit 
with only 
slightly higher variance. To avoid this inefficiency of choice, the trade
-off between risk and expected 
profit in the 
provider
Õs preferences could be explicitly taken into account, e.g. by applying a utility 
function as an objective function.
 The
 approach was evaluated
 by investigating
 the required amount of available monitoring data and 
identified 
and 
suggests 
20 observations for each SLA combination.
 This
 amount of data is required in 
case that all monitoring data is treated equal and no prefere
nces for the age of the monitoring data are 
included. 
In order to 
investigate the impact 
of changing violation probability distributions that could 
be caused e.g. by an extension of the infrastructure
, the measure of ris
k has to be adapted in order to 
weight more recent data higher than older data. This could be achieved by an approach like 
exponential smoothing
 (Trigg a
nd Leach 1967)
.  Further future work includes the evaluation of an extension of the risk
-aware decision support in a 
scenario with a se
rvice intermediary. 
The intermediary on the one hand
 has to make a decision 
analogously to the one presented in the work at hand
 about the 
SLAs
 that he wants to offer to 
consu
mers. On the other hand, the intermediary does not p
rovide all of the services by
 themsel
f but 
requests services from providers
 and hence, has to select providers and SLAs for those services.
