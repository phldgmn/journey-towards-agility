 Agile Software Assurance:
 An Empirical Study 
Noura Abbas School of Electronics and Computer Science, University of Southampton Southampton, SO17 1BJ, United Kingdom n.abbas@ecs.soton.ac.ukAbstract 
This poster will describe an empirical study of Agile 
Software Assurance. The main goal of this research is 

to study the quality of Agile projects in order to help 

software development organizations to have deeper 

understanding of Agile Methods, principles and 

practices. Moreover, this research will help evaluating 

the quality of Agile projects. 
1. Research Questions This Study will help to answer the following 
research questions:  
1.What is the quality of Agile Software, 
importantly, how we can evaluate the quality of 

Agile Software?  
2.Can Agile assure the quality under time pressure 
and with unstable requirements? 
3.What are the best ways to assure the quality of 
Agile Software? 
4.Different organizations interpret words like 
Agile and iterative in different ways. This leads 

to another question: how do organizations 

understand these terms? 
2. Related Work There have been many initiatives relating Agile 
with quality standards such as CMMI and ISO 9000. 

Although the results of thes
e initiatives vary, they all 
shared the same idea of modifying Agile methods in 

order to be accepted from the standards point of view 

[1, 2, 3]. 3. Methodology, Results and Future Work 
Interviews and questionnaires constitute the 
appropriate qualitative and quantitative research 
methods to collect as much data as possible about 
Agile in practice. We have started interviewing people 

from four organizations, and have talked with fifteen 

individuals who had experience with Agile projects.

The interviewees are involved in different roles within 

the organizations. The projects were chosen from a 

range of software development organizations. 
 In addition, we have developed two questionnaires 
which will be used to evaluate 10 projects that use 

Agile. The first questionnaires will be to understand 

what organizations mean by the term “Agile”, and how 

Agile they are.Then the second one will be to find out 

how organizations assure the quality of Agile software 

products, and what are the reasons behind their success.  
Our initial findings showed that the final products 
had different levels of quality. In addition, 

organizations use different Agile practices to assure the 

quality: testing, re-factoring, pair programming, 

recruiting skilled people, and continuous 

communication with the customer. Furthermore, the 

results showed that organizations prefer to adopt Agile 

practices rather than following one particular method. 

Importantly, those organizations have different 

understanding of Agility, and probably this is one of 

the important reasons behind having “Agile” software 

with different levels of quality.  
The future work will be to analyze the collected 
data, in order to understand how people used Agile;

and to find out how this usage affects the quality of the 

final products. The final stage will be to bring all the 

findings together and inform the model which will 

describe how to improve the quality of Agile projects.
