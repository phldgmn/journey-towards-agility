 ABSTRACT  
This paper analyses the relationships between 
personality, team 
processes, task characteristics
, product quality and 
satisfaction in 
software development teams. Th
e data analysed here were 
gathered from a sample of 35 team
s of students (105 participants) 
from a Spanish university. These teams applied an adaptation of 
an agile methodology, eXtreme Programming (XP), to develop 
the same software product. 
We found that the teams with the 
highest job satisfaction
 are 
precisely the ones whose members score highest for the 
personality factors 
agreeableness
 and 
conscientiousness. The 
satisfaction levels are also higher when the members can decide 
how to develop and organize their work. On the other hand, the 
level of satisfaction
 and 
cohesion
 drops the more 
conflict 
there is
 between the team members. Finally, the teams exhibit a 
significant positive correlation 
between the personality factor 
extroversion
 and software product
 quality. Categories and Subject Descriptors D.2.9 [Software Engineering
]: Management Œ 
programming 
teams.  General Terms Management, Experimentation. Keywords:
 Personality factors; Team processes; Task 
characteristics; Software
 quality; Satisfaction 
1. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN  
This paper describes an experimental study that analysed the 
relationships between the bi
g five personality factors 
(neuroticism, extroversion
, openness to experience
, agreeableness and conscientiousness
) at team level, the task characteristics 
(autonomy and 
interdependency
) and the team processes 
(cohesion and 
conflict
) for the purpose of evaluating their 
relationship to the quality of the 
developed software and the mean 
satisfaction of the team members in an academic setting with 
undergraduate students developing so
ftware using an adaptation 
of the agile XP method. It follows in the research footsteps of 
Barry at al. [1] that related personality to individual and group 
performance. This empirical study 
run fits the description of a 
quasi-experiment [3]. Quasi-e
xperiments are run when the 
subjects cannot be randomly a
ssigned to an experimental 
condition or, alternatively, a treatment cannot be randomly 

assigned to a group. Statistical
 tests, like correlations and 
regressions, are applied to the data collected, as applicable. This 
study follows the guidelines for 
reporting empirical research in 
software engineering [6] 
as closely as possible. 
1.1  Variables and Instrumentation Input and process measures (
personality, cohesion, conflict, 
interdependency, autonomy
 and 
satisfaction) were collected at the 
individual level and aggregated to
 the mean level. The team™s 
professor graded the deliverabl
es, and then we assessed the 
quality of the software product.  
1. Personality. The NEO FFI test was used to evaluate the team 
member personality [4]. This test
 is composed of 60 questions 
that measure the five persona
lity factors: neuroticism, 
extroversion, openness to experience, agreeableness and 
conscientiousness.  
2. Cohesion and conflict
. 13-item questionnaire for evaluating 
team processes (cohesion and c
onflict) taken from the Gross 
Cohesion Questionnaire [9] and an Intragroup Conflict Scale 
[7]. Conflict is divided into 
social conflict
 and task conflict
. 3. Interdependency 
and autonomy
. The questionnaire used to 
determine the task characteristics (interdependency and 
autonomy) is composed of 12 
items. Task interdependency is 
determined following van der Vegt et al. [10], whereas task 
autonomy is measured using Molleman™s questionnaire [8]. 
4. Satisfaction
. Gladstein™s questionnaire was used to evaluate 
satisfaction. Specifically, team satisfaction was evaluated 
using three items from Gladstein 
[5], indicating how satisfied 
people claim to be with their 
team mates, working together, 
etc. This is the response vari
able used in the regression 
analyses. 
5. Software Quality. To measure the quality of the software 
product, the professor analysed the code and project 
documents. Also the professor recorded the progress made in 
the exercises to quantitatively document each team members™ 

participation. We calculated the weighted grade on a scale 
from 0 to 10 points according to the following formula:

1.2  Participants and Subjects The participants were second-
year computing students at 
Universidad Autónoma de Madrid
™s Higher Technical School of 
Computing during the 2004/05 academic year. These students 
were taking the Data Structur
es and Algorithms course. The 
project set was to design and implement a distributed library 

management system. Developm
ent took place throughout the 
whole semester and professors ac
ted as users. The size of the 
resulting products ranged from 87.15 to 130 adjusted function 
points. A total of 105 students participated
 in this empirical study, of 
which 83 were male (80%) and 22 
were female (20%). Of the 
students, 75% (79 students) were 
aged less than 21 years and 25% 
(26 students) were from 21 to 30 
years of age. The students were 
divided into 35 three-member teams who worked together to 
develop the software. Each three-member development team is a 
subject of this quasi-experiment. These teams were formed at 
random and their members were b
lind to the quasi-experimental 
conditions. Teams used a common agile methodology (an 
adaptation of XP [2] to the academic environment) and the C 
programming language. 1.3  Data Collection Procedure 
All the questionnaires used were anonymised. The teams were 
identified by a team number and the participants by a team 
member number (1, 2 or 3). This subject and participant 
identification was the same for 
all the questionnaires at each 
measurement time. Information was gathered by means of 
measurements taken before, during and after project development. 
Before refers to when the project kicks off, the time during which 
the teams are being set up, but no
 team work has yet been done. 
At this time the NEO FFI 
personality test was handed out and 
collected in. 
During 
is the period, according to estimated project 
time, it takes to complete 45% of the development project (week 
11). The 
cohesion and conflict
 questionnaires were handed out 
and collected in. Finally, 
after 
is, according to estimated project 
time, when the project is 95% complete (week 23) just before 
project development is finished. 
The questionnaires handed out at 
this point measure the task characteristics (
interdependency and 
autonomy) and the 
satisfaction
 of the teams after their team work.  
1.4  Quasi-Experiment Validity 
Before running the quasi-exper
iment, we identified three main 
internal validity threats: 
1. Process conformance. This was mitigated by allocating two 
professors to every 60 students. These professors were 
responsible for monitori
ng project development. 
2. Knowledge of XP and development experience
. This was 
mitigated by forming teams at random and organizing a 
training session on the XP me
thodology applied to project 
development. 3. Software project to be developed
. To prevent this threat, all 
the teams undertook the same project.  
For results to be generalized to
 an industrial setting, special-
purpose quasi-experimental studi
es at software developer 
organizations would have to 
be planned and designed.

 3. CONCLUSIONS  
On the basis of the results obtai
ned in this quasi-experiment, we 
can say that the teams most 
satisfied with their job are precisely 
the ones that score higher on the personality factors of 
agreeableness
 and 
conscientiousness
. The levels of 
satisfaction are also higher when the team members can decide on how to 
develop and organize their work. 
On the contrary, the level of 
satisfaction and 
cohesion drops the greater 
task conflict
 is among 
team members. Finally, there is a significant correlation between 
an average score for the personality factor 
extroversion
 and 
evaluated software product 
quality
. Consequently, 
extroversion
 should be considered as a valid 
predictor of software quality fo
r developing software following an 
agile methodology. A high inter
action among team members is 
essential for this development me
thod. All participants could be 
classed as project mana
gers and are all responsible for the success 
or failure of the developed product. This way, traits like 
sociability, talkativeness, communicativeness, affability and 
openness appear to be conducive to the development of high 
quality software, as well as to the satisfaction of the development 
team members.
